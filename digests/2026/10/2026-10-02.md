## 1. [OmniSeek: Native Tool Integration for Multi-turn Audio-Visual Reasoning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02181)

**Authors:** Haibo Wang, Jiteng Mu, Jialu Li, Jingru Yi +4 more  
**Categories:** cs.CV · **Published:** 2026-10-01 · **Relevance:** 15

TL;DR: OmniSeek turns a large language model into an active agent that can decide when to look at video frames or listen to audio, pulling only the needed snippets from long recordings to answer questions. By training on synthetic multi‑hop reasoning paths and fine‑tuning with reinforcement learning, it learns to gather evidence from both modalities and avoid shortcuts, boosting performance on audio‑visual benchmarks.  
Why it matters: It enables more efficient, accurate reasoning over long audio‑visual streams, which is critical for real‑world AI systems that must process continuous multimodal data.

## 2. [KaliBench: A Fine-Grained Benchmark for Cybersecurity Tool Use on Kali Linux with Runtime-Free Verifiable Rewards](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02206)

**Authors:** Pengfei Li, Naufal Suryanto, Sicheng Zhang, Muzammal Naseer  
**Categories:** cs.CL, cs.AI, cs.CR · **Published:** 2026-10-01 · **Relevance:** 12

TL;DR: KaliBench is a new dataset of 8,504 real‑world Kali Linux command‑line tasks that tests whether language models can produce exact, executable commands from natural‑language queries. It shows that even large open‑weight models struggle, achieving at most 42% exact accuracy, but fine‑tuning with KaliBench‑derived rewards can bring smaller models up to the level of a 685B MoE model.  
Why it matters: The benchmark provides a realistic, reproducible way to evaluate and improve LLMs for practical cybersecurity automation, where command‑line precision is critical.

[Abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02206) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.02206)
