## 1. [AgentDoxx: Agentic Re-identification of Anonymized Text with Web Search](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.05586)

**Authors:** Jianing Wen, Tianshi Li  
**Categories:** cs.CR, cs.AI, cs.CY · **Published:** 2026-10-04 · **Relevance:** 12

TL;DR: Large language models that can browse the web can link anonymized interview transcripts to real people, with over 88% success once the target appears in a search result. Even models that refuse to name the person still retrieve the subject in up to 62% of cases, showing that privacy protection is fragile.  
Why it matters: This work quantifies the real‑world re‑identification risk of web‑enabled LLMs and highlights the need for stronger anonymization and privacy safeguards in AI systems.

[Abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.05586) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.05586)

## 2. [Have I Scene This Before? Spatially Grounded Conversational Memory for Complex Queries in Egocentric Assistants](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.05526)

**Authors:** Jiazhou Liang, Liam Gallagher, Kiko Chen, David Guo +3 more  
**Categories:** cs.AI · **Published:** 2026-10-04 · **Relevance:** 11

TL;DR: SpaC-MEM stores egocentric video history as a 3‑D object‑centric memory, letting an assistant answer long, spatially grounded questions without re‑processing the whole video. It outperforms video‑only baselines on a new Ego‑SpaCR benchmark while using far fewer tokens.  
Why it matters: It shows how to keep spatial context in a compact, updatable memory so assistants can handle complex, long‑term queries efficiently.

## 3. [Measurement-First Auditing of Agentic Leaderboards: Contamination Susceptibility, Matched-Control Re-evaluation, and Scorer Validation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.05830)

**Authors:** Dishu Yang, Qi Su, Hongbo Qin, Hansong Zhang  
**Categories:** cs.AI · **Published:** 2026-10-05 · **Relevance:** 10

TL;DR: The paper introduces a systematic audit method that classifies potential contamination in AI leaderboards into three distinct channels—training exposure, evaluation retrieval, and pipeline leakage—requiring different evidence levels. Applying this framework to a specific benchmark gap, the study finds no definitive evidence of contamination, with statistical analyses showing the gap could be due to chance and the scorer used failing validation.  
Why it matters: It provides a practical, evidence‑based protocol for AI engineers to rigorously assess and rule out contamination in leaderboard evaluations, ensuring more trustworthy benchmark results.

[Abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.05830) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.05830)

## 8. [Beyond Semantic Similarity: Performance and Costs of Agentic Retrieval for Complex Tasks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.05750)

TL;DR: Agentic retrieval, which lets an LLM reason while a retriever explores data, outperforms standard dense retrieval by 8.7 nDCG@10 points and works well on both ViDoRe v3 and BRIGHT tasks. It does so at a high cost: each query takes about 107 s and uses over 760 K input tokens.

## 10. [SALUS: Automated Auditing of NL-to-SQL Benchmarks through Weak Supervision of Multi-Agent Output](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.05540)

**Authors:** Shiyuan Zhou, Ashwin Gerard Colaco, Sainyam Galhotra, Sharad Mehrotra  
**Categories:** cs.DB, cs.CL, cs.LG · **Published:** 2026-10-04 · **Relevance:** 7

TL;DR: SALUS automatically finds wrong annotations in NL‑to‑SQL benchmarks by letting several LLMs generate SQL, then using their noisy votes to train a model that predicts which annotations are likely wrong. It achieves high accuracy (F1≈0.92) and shows that about 30–40% of popular benchmarks contain errors.  
Why it matters: It lets researchers reliably evaluate NL‑to‑SQL models and avoid misleading performance numbers caused by faulty benchmark data.
