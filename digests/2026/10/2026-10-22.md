## 4. [Real PMIDs, Wrong Papers: Harness Design for Biomedical Claim-Verification Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29812)

**Authors:** Mohammed Saqlain  
**Categories:** cs.AI, cs.CL, cs.IR, cs.LG · **Published:** 2026-10-21 · **Relevance:** 9

TL;DR: Using PubMed as the sole tool, letting a model craft its own queries boosts biomedical claim‑verification accuracy to 62% (vs. 49‑59% for other setups) and cuts search usage in half. The study shows retrieval quality, not reasoning, is the main bottleneck, and that closed‑book models often cite non‑relevant papers.  
Why it matters: Engineers can design more efficient, accurate scientific assistants by enabling models to generate targeted PubMed queries rather than relying on single‑shot retrieval or closed‑book answers.

[Abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29812) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.29812)

TL;DR: The paper shows that popular tricks for improving Retrieval Augmented Generation (RAG) don’t always add up when used together; a method that works well alone can lose most of its benefit in a full pipeline, and the best mix of techniques changes as the knowledge base grows.

## 7. [Multimodal Embodied Agents: A Unified Capability-centric Perspective from Computer-Use to Robot-Use](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30108)

**Authors:** Yanzhe Chen, Ziyi Yang, Jifeng Zhu, Qiming Huang +13 more  
**Categories:** cs.RO, cs.AI, cs.CV, cs.MA · **Published:** 2026-10-21 · **Relevance:** 8

TL;DR: The paper introduces PAPAV, a framework that breaks down multimodal embodied agents into five core capabilities—Perceive, Anticipate, Plan, Act, Verify—highlighting how moving from computer‑use to robot‑use changes the difficulty of each step. It shows that most existing benchmarks focus almost exclusively on the Act step, leaving Anticipate and Verify largely untested, and argues that future work should evaluate all five capabilities to build more reliable physical agents.  
Why it matters: By shifting evaluation from overall task success to a fine‑grained, capability‑centric view, engineers can better diagnose and improve the specific weaknesses that arise when agents transition from virtual to real‑world environments.

[Abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30108) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.30108)

## 8. [Circuits of LLM Classifiers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30076)

**Authors:** Hadi Khalaf, Sajani Vithana, Kunal Talreja, Haewon Jeong +2 more  
**Categories:** cs.LG · **Published:** 2026-10-21 · **Relevance:** 8

TL;DR: The paper proposes “circuits” that adaptively sequence and combine prompts to large language models, treating each prompt as a noisy measurement of a true label. By optimizing the order and stopping rule, the method achieves higher accuracy for a given average number of queries than simple routing or cascading approaches.  
Why it matters: It offers a principled way to trade off cost and reliability when using LLMs for classification, improving performance on diverse real‑world tasks.

[Abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30076) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.30076)

## 9. [What the Author Wrote, What the Agent Reads: Silent Cue Loss in Enterprise Document Ingestion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30026)

**Authors:** Yikai Zhao, Saurabh Pandey, Pradeep Kumar Misra  
**Categories:** cs.CL, cs.AI, cs.IR · **Published:** 2026-10-21 · **Relevance:** 8

TL;DR: Converting documents to text for AI agents silently drops formatting cues like highlights and colours that authors use to signal important requirements, causing agents to confidently miss or misinterpret key policy points. The loss is severe—F1 scores drop from ~0.69 to ~0.15 or lower—across multiple models and formats.  
Why it matters: Fixing the ingestion step to preserve these visual cues can dramatically improve the reliability of enterprise AI agents without costly changes to the models themselves.

[Abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30026) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.30026)

## 10. [Bridging Foundational and Agentic Capabilities of LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29751)

**Authors:** Wei Guo, Yuanjian Xu, Jianing Hao, Yongyao Duan +3 more  
**Categories:** cs.CL, cs.SE · **Published:** 2026-10-21 · **Relevance:** 8

TL;DR: The paper introduces a new framework that separates a language model’s basic benchmark abilities from how it actually performs when used as an agent in real tasks. By comparing “foundational profiles” (benchmark scores) with “operation profiles” (real‑world interaction data), the authors show that benchmark performance alone can’t predict agent success and that the new framework pinpoints where models fall short.  
Why it matters: It gives AI engineers a practical tool to diagnose and improve LLM agents by focusing on the specific steps where they deviate from expected behavior, rather than relying on vague overall scores.

[Abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29751) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.29751)
