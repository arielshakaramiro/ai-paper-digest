## 1. [NavHarness: Adaptive Goals for Agentic Vision-Language Navigation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39915)

**Authors:** Haoxiang Shi, Zaijing Li, Muhe Ding, Xiang Deng +2 more  
**Categories:** cs.CV, cs.RO · **Published:** 2026-09-30 · **Relevance:** 12

TL;DR: NavHarness splits a long navigation task into short, verifiable sub‑goals, letting an agent check each goal’s completion before moving on. This reduces the amount of past context the agent must process and keeps the agent’s actions aligned with the overall instruction.    
Why it matters: By verifying and compressing local goals, NavHarness improves real‑world navigation accuracy while cutting inference cost, making it practical for deployment on resource‑constrained robots.

## 10. [From DNA Design to DNA Slimming: Auditable Agentic Discovery of a Deletion-Only Designer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.40143)

**Authors:** Joel Shor  
**Categories:** cs.LG, cs.NE, q-bio.GN · **Published:** 2026-09-30 · **Relevance:** 8

**TL;DR:**    
The paper introduces a new “slimming” task that removes unnecessary bases from a DNA sequence while keeping its predicted function, and builds a benchmark to evaluate such deletion-only designers. Using an AI agent called ERA, the authors automatically modify an existing substitution‑only designer to create a slimmer version (GRADASLIM) that outperforms random and greedy baselines on five transcription‑factor binding targets.  
**Why it matters:**    
Efficiently trimming DNA sequences can reduce vector size, synthesis cost, and assay complexity, making engineered genetic parts more practical for real‑world applications.
