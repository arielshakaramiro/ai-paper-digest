## 1. [P2Patch: Exploit-Guided Vulnerability Repair](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20245)

**Authors:** Mohsen Iranmanesh, Sina Moradi Sabet, Sina Marefat, Ali Javidi Ghasr +6 more  
**Categories:** cs.CR, cs.AI, cs.SE · **Published:** 2026-10-14 · **Relevance:** 12

TL;DR: P2Patch uses a two‑step adversarial loop—an exploit‑generating agent first creates a proof of vulnerability, then a patching agent fixes the code, and the exploit agent tries again with a different exploit. This process uncovers hidden weaknesses that single‑pass repair misses, improving real‑world fix coverage from 78% to 85% on held‑out tests.  
Why it matters: By forcing patches to survive independent, varied attacks, P2Patch delivers more robust, security‑verified fixes that are less likely to leave exploitable gaps.

[Abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20245) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.20245)

## 2. [Same Claim, Different Interpretations: How LLM Agents Resolve Ambiguity in Scientific Claims](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20457)

**Authors:** Delip Rao, Ahmed Muharram, Chris Callison-Burch  
**Categories:** cs.CL · **Published:** 2026-10-14 · **Relevance:** 10

TL;DR: The study shows that large‑language‑model agents often make hidden assumptions when evaluating scientific claims, and making those assumptions explicit (via “Claim Cards”) changes their verdicts only when the assumptions are deliberately altered.  
Why it matters: Explicitly recording a verifier’s interpretation lets engineers trace disagreements, reduce wasted computation, and design more reliable AI‑assisted scientific validation.

## 5. [One Task, Many Interfaces: Simple Cross-Interface Consistency Training Improves LLM Agent Robustness](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20576)

**Authors:** Artin Tajdini, Manpreet Kaur, Peng Qi  
**Categories:** cs.LG, cs.AI, cs.CL · **Published:** 2026-10-14 · **Relevance:** 8

TL;DR: Training large language model agents with a self‑supervised consistency loss that forces the same action distribution under two different interface styles (e.g., renamed actions) makes them more robust to interface changes. The method, Cross‑Interface Policy Regularization (CIPR), boosts accuracy on unseen interface perturbations by about 10% without hurting performance on the original interface.  
Why it matters: CIPR lets deployed LLM agents tolerate changes in API or command syntax, reducing the need for costly retraining when interfaces evolve.

[Abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20576) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.20576)

## 7. [Price of Anarchy of First-Price Auctions in Agentic Markets](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20587)

**Authors:** Shubhendu Trivedi, Jacob Eisenstein, Alekh Agarwal, Ian Gemp +2 more  
**Categories:** cs.GT, cs.AI, cs.LG · **Published:** 2026-10-14 · **Relevance:** 7

TL;DR: First-price auctions in AI‑agent markets can still achieve near‑optimal welfare if many agents value a prompt similarly (competitive floor) or if prompt difficulty largely determines value (near‑independence). The paper shows the price of anarchy can drop to zero with a high floor or to about 0.86 of optimal when values are nearly independent.  
Why it matters: It gives practical design guidelines for prompt‑routing systems, proving that simple first‑price auctions can be efficient without needing complex truth‑telling mechanisms.

[Abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20587) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.20587)
