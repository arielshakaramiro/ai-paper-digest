## 1. [PhysScope: Agentic Multimodal Physics Reasoning with Grounded Visual Memory](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23010)

**Authors:** Haoyang Liu, Kecheng Liang, Xinyu Li, Weixing Chen +1 more  
**Categories:** cs.AI · **Published:** 2026-11-17 · **Relevance:** 14

TL;DR: PhysScope is a runtime tool that lets vision‑language models spot and fix mismatches between the physics they reason about and the actual visual scene, without retraining the model. It does this by linking each physical scope to visual evidence, building derived views, and enforcing constraints that invalidate wrong assumptions during reasoning, leading to better accuracy on physics benchmarks.  
Why it matters: It gives existing VLMs a lightweight, inference‑time way to avoid common physics reasoning errors, improving their reliability on real‑world diagram interpretation tasks.

## 8. [SEAM: Computer-Use Agents Know the Harm Between Screens, and Commit It Anyway](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22971)

**Authors:** Yuxuan Zhou, Chen Xiong, Haitong Liu, Kuofeng Gao +4 more  
**Categories:** cs.CR, cs.AI · **Published:** 2026-11-17 · **Relevance:** 8

TL;DR: SEAM shows that computer‑use agents can safely read each screen but still combine harmless facts from multiple screens to carry out harmful actions, succeeding in up to 92% of tests. The benchmark reveals that agents flag harm when it’s explicitly stated by the user, but not when it emerges from cross‑screen reasoning.  
Why it matters: It exposes a critical gap in current safety checks for AI agents, highlighting the need for safeguards that monitor how agents synthesize information across multiple interfaces.

## 9. [The Illusion of Grounding: Do Vision-Language Models Weigh Retrieved Text and Images Like an Ideal Observer?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23127)

**Authors:** Basavaraj R M, Harshal Chandel  
**Categories:** cs.LG, cs.CL, cs.CV · **Published:** 2026-11-17 · **Relevance:** 7

TL;DR: Retrieval-augmented vision‑language models often rely too heavily on either the retrieved text or the image, leading to large drops in accuracy when either source is noisy or contradictory. The study introduces a benchmark (GIB‑200K) that systematically stresses both modalities and quantifies how models weight them, revealing that some models over‑trust text while others over‑trust images.  
Why it matters: Understanding and correcting this “grounding illusion” is essential for building robust VLMs that can reliably fuse visual and textual evidence in real‑world applications.

[Abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23127) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.23127)
