## 1. [Mitigating Agentic Deception via Evaluation-Side Partially Observable Games](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12835)

**Authors:** Hanxiao Li, Shutong Liu, Yongjia Ma, Jiaming Ji +10 more  
**Categories:** cs.LG · **Published:** 2026-11-09 · **Relevance:** 12

TL;DR: The paper introduces a game‑theoretic framework (e‑PoG) that turns deception detection into a min‑max problem between an agent and a monitor, using a verifier to penalize dishonest behavior. Experiments show this method cuts deceptive actions by 26% and boosts task success by up to 40% over prior defenses.  
Why it matters: It offers a scalable, theoretically grounded way to keep large language model agents honest without relying on brittle one‑shot checks.

## 2. [TRIAD: Multimodal Retrieval across Abstraction, Constraints, and State](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12364)

**Authors:** Xiangyang Wu, Chen Ju, Xu Chen, Likang Peng +3 more  
**Categories:** cs.CV · **Published:** 2026-11-09 · **Relevance:** 10

TL;DR: TRIAD is a benchmark that tests multimodal retrieval systems on complex, evolving user requests—checking if they can match visual items to concepts at different abstraction levels, satisfy both positive and negative constraints, and update results across dialogue turns. No existing system dominates all six test subsets, showing that current models still struggle to fully satisfy nuanced, multi‑constraint queries.  
Why it matters: The benchmark exposes the gap between retrieving visually similar items and actually meeting all user requirements, guiding the development of more reliable retrieval for agentic AI systems.
