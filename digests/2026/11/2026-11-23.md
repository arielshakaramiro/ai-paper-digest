## 1. [Can Vision-Language Models Synthesize Executable Game-Playing Programs?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27741)

**Authors:** Yao Tong, Jiaxin Qi, Dingyu Zhang, Jianqiang Huang  
**Categories:** cs.AI · **Published:** 2026-11-20 · **Relevance:** 12

TL;DR: The paper introduces PlayScriptBench, a new benchmark that asks vision‑language models to write a full, executable program that can play a game from start to finish, rather than just picking actions one step at a time. Experiments show that even strong VLMs fail to produce reliable game‑playing scripts, highlighting a gap between visual understanding and practical control.  
Why it matters: It reveals that current VLMs need much stronger program synthesis and long‑horizon reasoning skills to be useful for real‑time, cost‑effective autonomous game agents.

## 5. [When Visually Rich Documents Meet Multi-Vector Retrieval for Region-level Retrieval-Augmented Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27542)

**Categories:** cs.CV · **Published:** 2026-11-20 · **Relevance:** 9

TL;DR: MV-VisRAG improves Retrieval-Augmented Generation for visually rich documents by splitting pages into semantically meaningful regions, encoding each region with a vision‑language model, and using these compact vectors for efficient, late‑interaction retrieval. Why it matters: It enables faster, more precise AI responses on complex documents (e.g., PDFs, forms) by focusing on the right visual parts instead of whole pages.
